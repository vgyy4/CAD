# ADR 0040: CAM phase 1 — 2.5D pocket and profile toolpaths, G-code

Status: accepted (2026-11-04). PROMPT.md "Phase 3+": CAM after direct modeling.

## Context
Fusion's Manufacture workspace, FreeCAD Path/CAM and Onshape's CAM Studio start from the
same everyday operations on a router or mill: clear a pocket, cut a part free along its
outline, cut a hole along its wall, all with a flat end mill in depth passes. The hard part
is robust 2D offsetting with arcs and islands; the rest is bookkeeping and G-code.

## Decision
A new crate, `gusset-cam`, computes 2.5D toolpaths from planar regions and writes G-code:

* **Geometry**: `cavalier_contours` (MIT OR Apache-2.0, pure Rust, no unsafe) offsets closed
  polylines with exact arcs (bulges) and keeps islands: a sketch region's lines and arcs map
  to it one to one, circles to two half arcs, Bézier spans are sampled.
* **Operations**: `pocket` (the region shrunk by the tool radius, then again by the stepover
  until nothing is left; rings cut from the middle out, so the walls come last), and
  `profile_outside` / `profile_inside` (the boundary moved out or in by the radius; corners
  come out as arcs). Every ring is cut at every depth level (equal passes no deeper than the
  stepdown): rapid to its start at the safe height, plunge at the plunge feed, the ring, retract.
* **Drilling**: `drill` takes the sketch's circle centres (or the circle ids given), visits
  them nearest first and writes canned cycles: `G98 G81` (drill) or `G98 G83 … Q` (peck), `R` at
  the stock top, `G80` after.
* **Tabs**: a profile can leave `tabs: [count, width, height]`: on the passes below the tabs'
  top the ring rises over each tab (its width plus the tool diameter, evenly spaced, the ring
  split exactly, arcs too) and comes back down after it.
* **Entry**: by default each ring is entered on a helix along itself at 3 deg (as many turns as
  the depth needs; `ramp_deg: 0` plunges straight down), from the level above, then cut once
  flat at its depth.
* **G-code**: metric, absolute, XY plane (`G21 G90 G17 G94`, then `G40 G49 G80 G54` to clear
  compensation, offsets and cycles), `S… M3` (the spindle speed must be positive), rapids up
  before across, `G1`, and `G2`/`G3` with `I`/`J` centre offsets; an arc that rounds to nothing
  is written as a `G1`; `M5 M30`.
* **Review fixes (2026-11-04)**: a ramp on a tabbed profile stops at the tabs' top and plunges
  the rest at the ring's start (it cut through the tabs before); tabs need a count of 1–100
  and a height below the depth, and are refused on pockets and drilling; the stepover is
  at most 0.5 of the diameter (wider steps left ridges in narrow arms and cusps in corners;
  an extra ring only where the whole offset vanished did not cover arms that vanish first); Béziers are sampled to 0.002 mm; construction circles are not drilled; sizes,
  feeds and speeds are bounded; at most 20000 holes (the ordering is quadratic).
  Coordinates are the sketch's (its plane is z = 0).
* **Commands**: `cam.toolpath` (rings, depth levels, cut/plunge/rapid length and time) and
  `cam.export_gcode` (`.nc`/`.gcode`/`.ngc` in the project folder), taking the sketch, region
  ids, the operation, tool diameter, depth, stepdown, stepover, feeds, spindle speed, stock top
  and safe height. The regions come from `gusset_eval::sketch_regions`.
* **App**: a MANUFACTURE ribbon tab with the sketch tool, Toolpath and G-code export (their
  forms come from the commands).
* Limits: at most 1000 depth passes, 10 000 pocket rings and 5M moves.

## Consequences
* Unit tests check a pocket's extent (a tool radius inside), its depth levels, an outside
  profile's corner arcs, an inside profile's radius and a pocket keeping clear of an island;
  a commands test exports a pocket.
* Not in this phase: toolpaths stored in the document (they are recomputed from the command's
  values), climb/conventional choice, tool libraries,
  adaptive clearing, 3D surfacing, simulation and a toolpath preview in the viewport.

## Amendment (2026-11-04): preview

`cam.toolpath` takes `path: true` and then also returns the cutting moves (plunges included,
rapids left out) as polylines in world coordinates: the sketch frame, Z along its normal,
arcs sampled every 10 degrees, at most 200000 points (`path_truncated` when cut short). The
app asks for it, draws the last toolpath thin in the warning colour over the model, keeps
the path out of the inspection dialog, and drops it when the model changes. The path is not
stored in the document yet.

## Amendment (2026-11-05): climb milling

Rings are now oriented by the cut wanted, not by how the offset came out: climb milling
(the default; `conventional: true` for the other) keeps the material on the right of the
tool's travel with the spindle turning clockwise (M3). Material inside a ring (an outside
profile, an island's wall) means clockwise; material outside (a pocket's wall, an inside
profile) counter-clockwise. Before, outside profiles ran counter-clockwise, which is
conventional; pockets and inside profiles were already climb.

## Amendment (2026-11-07): jobs in the document, helical bores

`cam_job` is a feature with no body (like a datum plane): sketch, regions, operation, tool and
cutting values, lengths and the ramp angle as expressions. It lives in history like any other
feature and follows parameter changes; `cam_toolpath` / `cam_export_gcode` take `job: <name>`
instead of the fields (giving both is refused). Agents write jobs in the text format.
`gusset_cam::helical_bore` mills round holes up to twice the tool's diameter on a helix of
half-circle arcs (pitch at most the stepdown, or the ramp angle's), then one flat turn on the
floor; holes the tool's size are plunged, smaller or wider ones refused (operation `bore`: every
circle of the sketch, at its own diameter).


## Amendment (2026-11-07): tabs with ramps, drill retract plane, tidy G-code

Tabbed levels no longer plunge vertically into stock. With a ramp angle, the helix stops at the
tabs' top and the tool ramps the rest of the way down along the ring's first stretch before the
first tab; coming down after a tab is a ramp along the stretch before the next tab (steeper
if that stretch is shorter). The ramped stretch is then cut again at depth (back, then forward)
so every part of the ring outside the tabs is cut at full depth; no cutting move below the
tabs' top lies within a tab span. Without a ramp the plunge stays vertical. Canned drilling
cycles retract to R = top + min(1 mm, (safe - top) / 2), also between pecks. The G-code never
writes a line twice in a row or a rapid to where the tool already is, and has a
`(T1 D<d> flat end mill)` comment (no M6: many hobby controllers refuse it).

## Amendment (2026-11-07): adaptive clearing

`Operation::PocketAdaptive { stepover }` (new; `Pocket` is unchanged) clears a pocket so that
the tool's radial depth of cut, once it is cutting, stays at or below `stepover` x diameter:
no full-width cut through a corner or a slot. It keeps the offset-ring structure (rings from
the middle out, the wall last) and changes two things.

1. **Ring spacing.** A ring reaches `1 / sin(a / 2)` times further than the ring inside it at a
   convex corner of angle `a`, so rings are `stepover x sin(a / 2)` apart (`a` the sharpest
   convex line-line corner of the region; clamped to 60 degrees, so sharper corners still
   exceed the limit, up to 2x). A right-angled corner makes the spacing 0.71 x stepover.
2. **Slots by trochoidal loops.** A ring with a stretch that has no ring inside it nearby (the
   innermost ring of a branch, or the arm of a shape that narrows) encloses stock the ring would
   cut full width. Such a ring becomes a slot: circles of radius `rho = min(tool radius,
   ring depth - 0.01)` whose centres advance along the ring by `delta`, the largest advance for
   which the circle's leading edge, against the disc the previous circle cleared, bites no
   deeper than a straight cut of the stepover (closed form, bisection). The tool spirals out
   from a plunge at the first centre to the first circle, at the same pitch; with a ramp angle
   the descent is made along the spiral (and whole extra turns of the circle). Rings wholly
   inside a slot's cleared band (within `rho sin(a / 2)` of its path) are left out. A pocket
   too narrow for loops (`rho` under a quarter of the tool radius) keeps a plain ring: a
   full-width cut is then unavoidable.

Measured by walking the toolpath over a fine grid (tests): on a rectangle and an L the largest
radial depth equals the stepover (grid resolution 0.1 mm) and the whole reachable area is cut,
where the plain pocket cuts the full diameter. Limits: the plunge and the first spiral turn
leave the tool in its own hole, a short stretch that is cut at more than the stepover; the
contact angle at a convex corner can reach about 125 degrees although the radial depth stays
within the stepover (the cleared boundary there is the previous corner's circle, not a line);
islands are handled by the same offsets but untested. The gusset-commands field is not wired yet.

## Amendment (2026-11-07): rest machining

`Operation::PocketRest { stepover, previous_diameter }` (new; the other variants are unchanged)
clears what a pocket cut with a larger tool left, using the setup's (smaller) tool. The previous
tool must be larger than the setup's.

1. **What is left.** The previous tool (radius `R`) cleared the pocket shrunk by `R` and grown
   by `R` again (a morphological opening); the rest is the pocket minus that: corners sharper
   than `R`, arms and slots narrower than `2R`. The cleared area is grown by 1 um before it is
   taken away, so that its walls do not coincide with the pocket's (the boolean is unreliable on
   coincident edges). Pieces thinner than 0.05 mm (also numerical slivers) are skipped.
2. **Toolpath.** The small tool's centre must reach every place within its radius `r` of a
   piece, and stay in the pocket shrunk by `r` (no gouging). That area (each piece grown by `r`,
   intersected with the shrunk pocket) is cut as its outline plus offset rings inside it, a
   stepover apart, from the middle out. The outline partly runs in already cleared material
   (the tool only touches the piece): a little air cutting for exact reach. Depth levels,
   stepdown, ramps (a helix along each ring, as for `Pocket`), climb/conventional direction and
   G-code are those of `Pocket`; tabs are refused.
3. **Nothing left** (the previous tool reaches everything, e.g. a circular pocket) is
   `CamError::NothingToCut`, not an empty toolpath. `previous_diameter` not above the tool's, or
   a stepover outside (0, 0.5], is `CamError::Invalid`.

Measured on a grid (tests): in a 40 x 20 rectangle cleared with 10 mm and rest-machined with
3 mm, the rest pass is 8 % of the length of a full 3 mm pocket, leaves material only in the 3 mm
tool's own corner radius and keeps its disc inside the pocket; in an L whose vertical arm is
6 mm wide the arm is cleared. Limits: pockets with islands are refused (`Invalid`); the
gusset-commands field is not wired yet.

## Amendment (2026-11-07): 3D finishing

`raster_finish(surface, cutter, &RasterSpec, &Setup)` (new module `surface`; `Operation` and
`Setup` are unchanged, the gusset-commands field is not wired yet) finishes a triangle mesh
(`Surface3d`, Z up, tool from +Z) with parallel lines.

1. **Drop-cutter.** For a sample `(x, y)` the tool tip Z is the maximum, over the mesh points `p`
   within the tool radius `R`, of `p.z - h(|p_xy - (x, y)|)`, where `h` is the height of the
   cutter's underside above its tip: 0 (flat), `r - sqrt(r^2 - rho^2)` (ball), or the flat
   inside `R - c` and the corner arc of radius `c` outside it (bull nose; `Flat` is `c = 0`,
   `Ball` is `c = R`). Per triangle that function is concave (a plane minus a convex function), so
   its maximum is a vertex, an edge point or the facet contact: the facet contact is closed form
   (at `rho* = R - c + c m / sqrt(1 + m^2)` uphill, `m` the slope; the rim for a flat cutter),
   the edge contact is found by a ternary search over the part of the edge inside the radius.
   The result is exact for flat and ball cutters to rounding, and for the bull nose to the
   search precision (about 1e-12).
2. **Index.** Triangles are bucketed in a uniform XY grid (cells at least one tool radius, at
   most 512 per side); a sample looks at the cells under its disk and skips triangles whose
   highest point is not above the best tip so far.
3. **Path.** Lines are `stepover` apart at `angle_deg`, clipped to the bounds (the mesh's XY
   bounds by default, not grown by the tool radius), sampled at most `step` apart (divided
   evenly), thinned with Douglas-Peucker in the (distance, Z) plane within `tolerance`, linked
   in a zig-zag via the safe height. Z is raised by `stock_to_leave` (a rise in Z, not a normal
   offset) and never goes below `top - depth`; where no triangle is under the tool the path is at
   that bottom. A surface above the safe height is `CamError::Invalid`. More than 3 million
   samples, 1 million lines or too large a triangle index is `CamError::TooLarge`.
4. **Limits.** The holder and shank are not checked, the tool radius is not compensated in XY at
   the bounds, and there is no waterline, rest or steep/shallow separation.

Tested: flat plane (exact), 45 degree ramp with a ball (tip = ramp + r(sqrt 2 - 1), within
1e-6), a sphere cap (within the mesh sag, 1e-3), bull nose equal to flat / ball at its limits
and between them otherwise, and a brute-force gouge check against sampled mesh points.

## Amendment (2026-11-07): waterline and steep/shallow finishing

`waterline_finish(surface, cutter, &WaterlineSpec, &Setup)` and
`steep_shallow_finish(surface, cutter, &SteepShallowSpec, &Setup)` (module `surface::waterline`;
not wired into commands yet) add constant-Z and combined finishing on the same drop-cutter.

1. **Waterline.** The cutter-location (CL) field `CL(x, y)` = drop-cutter tip Z + `stock_to_leave`
   is sampled once on an XY grid (cell `sqrt(8 R tolerance)` clamped to `R/16 ..= R/2`, so the
   chord sag of a contour of radius R stays within `tolerance`; one layer of nodes outside the
   bounds is "free", so every contour closes). For each level `z = top - k stepdown` down to the
   bottom (`top - depth`, always cut last) the region `CL > z` is the part of the plane the tool
   tip cannot enter; its boundary is traced with marching squares. Each crossing is placed on its
   grid edge by 14 bisection steps on the true field (not by linear interpolation, which is
   wrong at the jump of a vertical wall) and the free-side end is kept, so a point never lies in
   the gouging region. Segments are oriented with the material on the right and chained into
   loops; loops are rotated to start nearest the previous end, simplified with Douglas-Peucker
   within `tolerance` and linked at the safe height. Climb keeps the material on the right
   (clockwise around a boss); conventional reverses. Levels at or above the highest CL have no
   contour and are skipped (`Toolpath::levels` lists the cut ones, `rings` counts loops). The
   default bounds are the mesh's XY bounds grown by the tool radius and one cell; explicit
   bounds clip the contours (a contour then runs along the box edge). More than 3 million grid
   nodes or 10000 levels is `CamError::TooLarge`; a surface above the safe height is `Invalid`.
2. **Steep/shallow.** Slope is the angle of the CL field's gradient (central differences
   `R/4` apart of the same Z the raster uses, never below the bottom, so walls and the mesh rim
   read as steep; by the envelope theorem it equals the surface slope at the contact). The
   waterline keeps only the contour points at or above `steep_deg` (default 45, `0 < a < 90`; an
   all-steep loop stays closed, otherwise the steep runs are cut as open polylines), and the
   raster keeps only runs of consecutive samples below it (single samples dropped). Waterlines
   are cut first, then the raster. `raster_finish` is unchanged; it shares the loop with a
   shallow-only filter.
3. **Fix.** `tri_drop` skips sliver triangles (twice the area under 1e-9 mm^2): the pole of a
   sphere mesh gave a garbage facet plane that the point-in-triangle tolerance accepted
   everywhere, lifting the CL near the apex.
4. **Limits.** Contour corners are as sharp as the grid (error up to one cell at a concave
   corner of the part); the holder is not checked; the steep/shallow seam has no overlap, so a
   one-sample strip at the threshold may be left to the neighbouring pass.

Tested: waterline around a cylinder boss with a flat cutter (every point at R + r from the axis,
one clockwise loop per level, the boss top level empty), around the plate below it (offset
square), around a hemisphere with a ball (radius `sqrt((R + r)^2 - (z + r)^2)`), a brute-force
no-gouge / no-float check for flat, ball and bull nose, errors and G-code; steep/shallow on a
dome on a plate (waterlines at the steep levels with analytic radii, no raster in the steep foot,
raster covers every shallow point within half a stepover) and its threshold extremes.

### Amendment: holder check and pencil finishing (3D)

**Holder.** `Holder { shank_diameter, flute_length, holder_diameter, stickout }` describes the
tool above the cutting end (tip to holder face = `stickout`; the shank spans `flute_length ..
stickout`). `check_holder(surface, cutter, &Setup, &Holder, &Toolpath) -> HolderReport` samples
every cutting move (lines and arcs, at most one tool radius apart, both ends included; rapids are
not checked). At a sample the highest surface point inside a cylinder's radius is the drop-cutter
of a flat cutter of that radius (same grid index); the part collides when that point is above
its lowest face (`tip + flute_length` for the shank, `tip + stickout` for the holder). The report
lists up to 10 000 collisions (`at` = tool tip, `part`, `depth`), the exact count
(`total_collisions`) and `min_clearance` (negative when colliding, infinity when nothing is
under the tool). Between samples nothing is looked at, so features narrower than a tool radius
can be missed. `raster_finish_safe` runs `raster_finish`, checks it and, if needed, raises the
tool via `lift_for_holder` to the lowest tip Z with no collision at each sample (arcs become
lines, a plunge's approach rapid is raised with it, above the safe height is `Invalid`). Raising
leaves material under the raised part of the path; `SafeRaster` reports `max_lift` and
`lifted_samples` so the user can re-fixture or use a longer tool instead. Not wired to commands.

**Pencil.** `pencil_finish(surface, Cutter::Ball, &PencilSpec { bitangency_angle_deg, tolerance,
bounds }, &Setup)` traces concave creases where the ball touches two surfaces. The CL field
(drop-cutter on a grid of r/8) is scanned in four directions for an upward turn of the profile
(`atan(slope after) - atan(slope before)`) of at least the bitangency angle; each hit is refined
by sampling the profile at 1/16 of the grid step and intersecting the straight pieces either
side of the kink (exact for planar facets), the height is a fresh drop-cutter. Points are
merged, chained by nearest neighbour (links up to 3 grid steps), singletons dropped, chains
simplified in 3D within `tolerance`. Smooth valleys (a fillet larger than the ball turns by only
about h/(R - r) per step), ridges and flat areas give nothing (`NothingToCut`); other cutters
are `Invalid`; more than 3 million grid nodes or 500 000 crease points is `TooLarge`. The angle
is the turning angle of the CL profile, the dihedral angle only when the crease is
perpendicular to the scan direction; creases within one grid step of the mesh boundary are
skipped. Tested: a 90 degree inside corner gives one straight line at tip height
`r (sqrt 2 - 1)` (1e-6), flat and fillet (R = 4r) and ridge give none.

**Amendment (2026-11-07, Run 9: boxes, vertical walls, speed).**

- *Contours and the box (F9-1).* The waterline field is `-inf` outside the box, so marching
  squares used to close a contour that leaves the box along the box edge, and the tool was
  dragged through the part (ball centre on the wall plane). Points on grid edges that cross the
  box edge are not points of the part's contour; they and the steps next to them are dropped, so
  a contour cut by the box becomes open runs that end on the box edge (box edges on the grid
  lines `bmin + k h` are exact; on the max side a run stops up to one grid cell short). Runs are
  linked by retract and rapid like any other pieces; nothing is ever cut along the box. A box
  that holds only the cut-off part of a contour (the part's own footprint as the box) gives
  `NothingToCut` at that level: the tool *centre* cannot reach the wall inside the footprint.
  The default box is still the mesh grown by the tool radius and a cell; an explicit box limits
  the tool tip (centre), it is not grown.
- *Vertical walls in pencil (F9-2).* At a floor/wall corner the CL field steps up (a vertical
  wall) or climbs at more than 80 degrees per grid step (a wall leaning by 0.1 degree), and the
  profile kink is not a straight-line corner. A node whose neighbour is higher by more than
  `tan 80` grid steps bisects the step on the true field (last position at the node's height),
  and the tip there is the crease. Rings (boss on a plate) are closed.
- *Support and ends (F9-3).* Every crease point must be held: the ball centre is
  `tip + r`, the mesh points at distance `r` (closest point on each triangle near the centre,
  within `0.5 % r`) must include two with normals at least the bitangency angle apart, and
  contacts on the mesh's free boundary (edges with one triangle after welding at 0.1 micron;
  a plate's rim, the end of a groove) do not count. This trims runs at the part's ends and
  rejects the spurious pieces of a faceted fillet. Chains shorter than 4 grid steps are dropped.
- *Speed (F9-6).* The ball-edge contact is closed form (the centre at distance `r` from the
  edge's line is a quadratic) instead of a 70-step ternary search per edge; bull-nose keeps the
  search but stops at 0.1 micron. Triangles whose XY box is farther than `r` from the sample are
  skipped. Dome R30 with a 80 mm box (4.7 k triangles, ball D6, stepdown 0.4): `steep_shallow`
  328 s -> 8.5 s, `waterline` 225 s -> 5.7 s (release). Shallow raster runs of
  `steep_shallow_finish` are now visited nearest-first (either end) instead of line by line.
  Not done: lower retract heights (the rapids still go to the safe height) and reuse of the
  last path for the G-code export.
